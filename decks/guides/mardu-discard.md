# Mardu Discard

Written 2026-10-11 by gpt-6.1-sol:high from the list at https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7954746, cards generated 2026-10-10. Sideboardless alpha: main deck only. Advice for the pregame; the board and card text win any disagreement.

## 1. Archetype and game plan

Mardu Discard is proactive creature aggro with a discard-and-graveyard engine, not a deck built to empty the opponent's hand. Its 60 cards comprise 22 lands, 26 creatures, and 12 other spells. Nine creatures cost one mana: four Marauding Mako, four Moonshadow, and one Cecil, Dark Knight.

Establish cheap threats, convert expendable cards into counters and damage, then finish through flying, menace, trample, or Practiced Offense. Turns four through six are the main closing window, not a guaranteed clock. Bloodghast and flashback provide staying power; Cool but Rude supplies reach when combat stalls. The deck wants productive discards rather than indiscriminately exhausting its hand.

## 2. Engine and key interactions

- **Discard conversion:** Marauding Mako gains one counter per discarded card. Moonshadow removes only one counter per event putting one or more permanent cards into your graveyard. Tersa Lightshatter costs {2}{R}; discarding two permanents together gives Marauding Mako two counters but removes only one from Moonshadow. Discarding Practiced Offense, Burst Lightning, or Erode does not grow Moonshadow.
- **Repeatable outlets:** Hardened Academic costs {R}{W}; each activation costs one discarded card and grants lifelink. Iron-Shield Elf costs {1}{B}; each activation discards a card, grants indestructible, and taps it. Neither activation costs mana. Separate activations discarding permanents can each trigger Moonshadow. Repeated lifelink or indestructible grants are redundant, but their discard payoffs are not.
- **Bloodghast loop:** Discard Bloodghast first, then play a land. Its return costs no mana. With Hardened Academic present, that departure from the graveyard produces a targeted counter. Save the land drop until after arranging this sequence. Returned Bloodghast cannot attack immediately unless its haste condition is satisfied.
- **Practiced Offense:** Cast for {2}{W}, or discard it and flash it back for {1}{W}. Flashback moves it out of the graveyard during casting, triggering Hardened Academic before the spell resolves. With one Academic, that means one targeted counter plus the spell's counter on every creature you control. Counters placed on Moonshadow cancel opposing -1/-1 counters one-for-one. Choose double strike for damage or lifelink to stabilize.
- **Cool but Rude:** Casting costs {1}{R}, reaching level 2 costs another {1}{R}, and reaching level 3 costs another {1}{R}: six mana total. Reach level 2 before attacking when possible. Each discard then deals 2 damage per level-2 Class; Tersa Lightshatter discarding two cards deals 4 per Class. Level 3 tutors before a random discard, so the tutored card is not protected. Multiple Classes provide separate attack-loot triggers.
- **Carnage, Crimson Chaos:** Discard it this turn, then cast it from the graveyard for mayhem {B}{R} during a legal creature-casting window. Mayhem does not grant flash. Its return ability can recover any other creature in this list, since their mana values are three or less. Returning Bloodghast is especially reusable: after its forced sacrifice, a later land can return it as a fresh object without Carnage's granted abilities.
- **Other refills:** Inti, Seneschal of the Sun rewards discarding with temporary access, not free casting. Casey Jones, Vigilante costs {1}{R}{R}, draws three immediately, then demands three random discards next upkeep. Tersa Lightshatter needs seven graveyard cards both when its attack ability triggers and when it resolves; its random exile can also trigger Hardened Academic.

## 3. The curve and sequencing by turn

**On the play:** Turn one, deploy Marauding Mako or Moonshadow; Cecil, Dark Knight is another option. Turn two, choose Hardened Academic for immediate flying pressure, Cool but Rude for repeatable filtering, or Bloodghast when {B}{B} is available. Turn three, attack with a developed board after Practiced Offense, or develop Tersa Lightshatter before combat. Alternatively, level Cool but Rude for {1}{R} and retain one mana for interaction. Turn four, combine a two-mana engine action with another spell. Turn five, prioritize lethal, flashback, or recursion over unnecessary deployment.

**On the draw:** Use the same opening curve, but reserve turn-two or turn-three mana for Erode or Burst Lightning when an opposing threat would win the race. Turn four often requires lifelink or defensive blocks before the turn-five finishing push.

Lead lands according to the actual color sequence. Early Concealed Courtyard, Inspiring Vantage, and Starting Town avoid forced tapped entries. Starting Town becomes tapped after your third turn; the two fastlands become tapped with three other lands already present. Blazemire Verge needs a Swamp or Mountain for red. Plan Bloodghast's {B}{B} and Hardened Academic's {R}{W} before spending life on shocklands.

## 4. Mulligans

Keep functional two- or three-land hands with an early creature, usable colors, and either an engine or meaningful follow-up. Two lands plus cheap interaction is usually better than four lands plus expensive refills. There are only four removal spells: two Burst Lightning and two Erode.

Ship hands with no early pressure, incompatible colors, or multiple Practiced Offense without creatures. One-land hands are speculative despite the low curve: attack-looting needs an attacker, and cycling Marauding Mako costs {2}. Do not treat Bloodghast as a reliable turn-two play without two black sources. On fewer cards, prioritize lands, a threat, and one coherent engine.

## 5. Combat and defense

Attack aggressively with disposable Bloodghast, flying Hardened Academic, and enlarged Moonshadow. Menace requires two blockers, but does not make unfavorable double-blocks safe. Inti, Seneschal of the Sun gives trample to an attacking creature, making Practiced Offense's double strike more effective against chumps.

Declare Iron-Shield Elf as a blocker before activating it: tapping an established blocker does not remove it from combat. Activating before attackers are declared prevents it from attacking. Bloodghast cannot block at all.

Hardened Academic can gain lifelink after blocks but before damage. Cecil, Dark Knight is a valuable deathtouch blocker, although its damage also costs you life. Once transformed, Cecil, Redeemed Paladin protects other attackers, not itself. A creature returned by Carnage, Crimson Chaos is sacrificed after dealing combat damage to a player; double strike can therefore sacrifice it after its first hit.

## 6. Resources and timing

Budget life as well as mana: shocklands, Starting Town, and Cecil, Dark Knight make races precarious. Use colorless Starting Town mana for generic costs when possible. Preserve {W} for Erode or {R} for Burst Lightning when interaction outweighs development. Kicked Burst Lightning costs {4}{R} total.

Activate Class levels and cast Practiced Offense before combat; neither is a combat trick. Keep discardable cards for creature protection and lifelink. With Casey Jones, Vigilante, use needed cards before the next upkeep where possible; that upkeep's random discard happens before your normal draw. Temporary cards from Inti, Seneschal of the Sun or Tersa Lightshatter still require mana and obey land-play limits.

## 7. What beats it and how to adapt

Exile removal, graveyard hate, and bounce disrupt recursion or erase accumulated counters. Indestructible does not answer exile, bounce, sacrifice, or toughness reduced to zero. Destroy-based sweepers punish wide deployment, although Iron-Shield Elf can survive by discarding. Retain a follow-up threat and avoid committing every creature when existing pressure suffices.

Larger blockers force reliance on evasion, trample, or Erode. Erode may ramp the opponent with a tapped basic, so remove blockers when that creates meaningful damage. Against faster aggro, prioritize lifelink and actual blockers rather than Bloodghast. Against control, preserve recurring threats and Cool but Rude instead of spending every resource on one oversized creature. No sideboard was supplied; adaptations here use the main deck.

## 8. Common mistakes

- Playing the land before discarding Bloodghast.
- Counting simultaneous permanent discards as multiple Moonshadow triggers.
- Expecting level-2 damage from earlier discards.
- Flashing back Practiced Offense during combat.
- Spending the final hand card without considering protection or the next land drop.
- Assuming random selection, mayhem, or temporary exile access bypasses costs and timing.
- Treating double strike without trample as automatic damage through a chump blocker.
