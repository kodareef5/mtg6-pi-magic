# Bant Airbending Combo

Written 2026-10-11 by gpt-6.1-sol:high from the list at https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7979917, cards generated 2026-10-10. Sideboardless alpha: main deck only. Advice for the pregame; the board and card text win any disagreement.

## 1. Archetype and game plan

Bant Airbending Combo is creature-ramp combo with a flying-midrange fallback. Its 60 cards comprise 24 lands and 36 nonlands, including 27 creatures. Four Llanowar Elves, four Bramble Familiar, and three Spider Manifestation provide eleven creature accelerators. Develop mana, assemble Doc Aurlock, Grizzled Genius with Appa, Steadfast Guardian and Aang, Swift Savior, then repeatedly cast the latter two from exile to create arbitrarily many Allies.

A clean accelerated opening can assemble the loop on turn four. Tokens normally win through combat on a subsequent turn, not immediately. Without the complete engine, flyers, repeated enters-the-battlefield effects, and Aang, Destined Savior's animated lands provide pressure. Protecting the engine matters more than racing with small mana creatures.

## 2. Engine and key interactions

**The token loop:** Have Doc Aurlock, Grizzled Genius and Appa, Steadfast Guardian on the battlefield. Cast Aang, Swift Savior from hand for {1}{W}{U}, targeting Appa with its entering trigger. Then:

- Cast the airbent Appa for {0}: its {2} alternative cost is reduced by Doc's {2} reduction.
- Resolve Appa's entering trigger, airbending Aang.
- Cast Aang from exile for {0}. Appa triggers to create an Ally; Aang's entering trigger airbends Appa.
- Repeat, retaining Doc on the battlefield. Each cycle produces one Ally.

Both creatures have flash, so this can happen on the opponent's turn. Let the relevant triggers resolve before continuing. Do not transform Aang until finished: the loop needs its front-face entering ability. Two copies of Appa alone do not substitute for Aang; the legend rule applies before entering triggers resolve.

**Mana extension:** An established Spider Manifestation can tap for {G} before each free Appa cast. Appa still has mana value four, so casting it untaps Spider Manifestation. Each cycle can therefore add another green mana. This requires Spider Manifestation to be able to use its tap ability; a newly entered one cannot immediately do so.

**Adventure conversion:** Airbend Bramble Familiar, then cast Fetch Quest using the {2} airbend cost, or {0} with Doc. This turns an inexpensive mana creature into a seven-card selection spell. After Fetch Quest resolves, its ordinary Adventure permission lets you cast Bramble Familiar, costing {G} with Doc, not automatically {0}: the earlier airbend permission no longer applies after the card changes zones.

**Finding pieces:** Michelangelo's Technique costs {4}{G}; Appa plus Doc fits its limit exactly, 4 + 2 = 6. Aang, Swift Savior plus Doc totals 3 + 2 = 5. Aang, at the Crossroads can find every creature here except another copy of itself. These effects put creatures onto the battlefield, so they trigger entering abilities but not Appa's cast-from-exile ability.

## 3. The curve and sequencing by turn

**On the play, a representative opening:**

- Turn one: untapped green source, Llanowar Elves for {G}.
- Turn two: second land; cast Doc Aurlock, Grizzled Genius for {G}{U}. Otherwise develop Bramble Familiar or Spider Manifestation for two mana.
- Turn three: third land plus Llanowar Elves supplies four mana; cast Appa, Steadfast Guardian for {2}{W}{W}. Its airbend is optional: leave essential support in place.
- Turn four: cast Aang, Swift Savior for {1}{W}{U} and begin the loop if interaction permits. With missing pieces, use Michelangelo's Technique or Aang, at the Crossroads at five mana.
- Turn five: attack with established tokens and flyers, or continue rebuilding and generating value.

**On the draw:** Use the same first two turns when safe, but prioritize bodies against pressure. Turn three may require holding {1}{W}{U} for Aang rather than tapping out for Appa. Turn four establishes Appa or searches; turn five seeks a protected loop or stabilizing attack. These are branches, not a requirement to delay an available safe combo.

Lead Breeding Pool or Temple Garden when their basic land types enable your next Verge. Breeding Pool enables both Verges; Temple Garden does too. Starting Town is untapped only on your first three turns, so deploy it early. Plan actual white sources for Appa's {W}{W}; creature mana mostly supplies green. Hallowed Fountain supports white-blue but cannot cast turn-one Llanowar Elves. Choose Cavern of Souls's type for the creature you most need to resolve, Ally covers both Aang cards and Appa, but not Doc.

## 4. Mulligans

Keep functional two- or three-land hands with early acceleration and either an engine piece or selection spell. Count colors, not just lands: Doc needs green-blue, Aang needs white-blue, and Appa needs two white. A two-land hand relying on an accelerator must still function if that creature dies.

Ship hands with expensive payoffs but no early development, multiple inaccessible colors, or redundant legends without a supporting engine. One-land hands are fragile even with Llanowar Elves. On a mulligan, retain mana and complementary pieces over a second Appa or speculative late-game transformation. Against aggression, a sturdy two-drop and usable white-blue interaction can outweigh maximum combo speed.

## 5. Combat and defense

Attack with flyers when they are not needed to block or protect the engine. Preserve Doc and active mana creatures unless trading prevents substantially more damage or lethal. Allies are expendable blockers; their purpose is to buy time or become a decisive army.

Aang's flash and Appa's flash allow surprise aerial blocks, but decide whether their entering triggers are needed elsewhere. Airbending an opposing creature is temporary relief: its owner can recast it for {2}. Do not treat it as permanent removal.

Michelangelo's Technique's sneak costs {3}{G} and returns an unblocked attacker during declare blockers. Keep four mana available without counting that attacker as a mana source. Returning an attacker also means giving up its combat damage. Transforming Aang through waterbend can use new tokens, but tapping those tokens reduces immediate blocking capacity.

## 6. Resources and timing

Jennifer Walters for {1}{W} protects your turn from opposing spells, not activated or triggered abilities. Resolve her before committing to a vulnerable combo when possible. The opponent can still respond while she is a spell or remove her through abilities afterward.

Interdimensional Web Watch costs {4} from hand and can immediately tap for two mana restricted to spells from exile. Its revealed cards expire at the end of your next turn. Lands use normal land-play limits; playing one does not trigger Appa. Doc discounts eligible spells but does not remove their colored requirements unless the chosen cost is entirely generic, as with airbend.

Keep {1}{W}{U} open for Aang to airbend a threatening spell or creature. Keep {2}{W}{W} for Appa to shelter your nonland permanents; this is exile followed by later recasting, not an immediate blink. Airbending Doc removes its discount until it returns.

Airbender Ascension offers a single end-step creature blink once it has four quest counters. The Mind Stone requires {5}{W} and tapping to harness before its recurring end-step blink becomes available. Both favor repeated entering effects rather than explosive immediate mana expenditure.

## 7. What beats it and how to adapt

Fast aggression punishes shock-land payments, Starting Town payments, and setup turns. Use blockers, limit unnecessary life payments, and accept a slower assembly. Removal aimed at Doc ends the free-casting loop; removal aimed at Appa stops token production. Sweepers punish an army that cannot yet attack.

Countermagic and restrictions on casting from exile attack the engine directly. Favor Jennifer Walters, appropriate Cavern of Souls choices, and entering creatures through selection spells. Against removal-heavy opponents, spread development and hold flash creatures rather than exposing everything. Graveyard hate alone does not stop an exile-based loop, although it can affect milled resources. No sideboard was supplied; adaptations here concern sequencing, not invented substitutions.

## 8. Common mistakes

- Treating airbend as unrestricted timing permission: nonflash spells still need their normal casting window.
- Airbending support pieces indiscriminately, especially Doc or an active Spider Manifestation.
- Expecting newly recast mana creatures to tap immediately.
- Counting Web Watch mana toward transformations, waterbend, or spells from hand.
- Treating cheated creatures as cast spells, or discounting their mana value.
- Expecting fresh Allies to attack immediately, or transforming Aang midway through the loop.
- Ignoring legend-rule losses, Verge prerequisites, or Starting Town entering tapped after turn three.
