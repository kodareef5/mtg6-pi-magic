# Izzet Aggro

Written 2026-10-11 by gpt-6.1-sol:high from the list at https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7980848, cards generated 2026-10-10. Sideboardless alpha: main deck only. Advice for the pregame; the board and card text win any disagreement.

## 1. Archetype and game plan

Izzet Aggro is a haste-and-burn tempo deck: establish cheap pressure, remove or bounce blockers, and finish with evasive attacks or direct damage. Its best draws threaten decisive damage around turns four through six, but 24 lands, graveyard reuse, and exile-based follow-ups support longer games.

Hired Claw starts the clock; Colorstorm Stallion turns subsequent spells into combat damage; Nova Hellkite supplies immediate airborne pressure. Scalding Viper makes opposing stabilization costly. Against creature decks, spend early damage clearing profitable attacks. Against slower decks, preserve burn for lethal rather than automatically targeting creatures. The deck has no counterspells: keeping mana open represents damage or Bounce Off, not protection from every opposing play.

At five mana, Stallion plus two unkicked Burst Lightning costs 3 + 1 + 1 = 5: an unblocked Stallion attacks for 5, and the spells can deal another 4. A kicked Burst Lightning costs {4}{R}, deals 4, and triggers both the pump and a Stallion copy. The token has haste but does not copy the temporary pump or trigger from the spell already cast; normally the original attacks as a 4/4 and the new token as a 3/3.
- **Stingcaster Mage recursion:** Mage costs {1}{R}; flashing back Burst Lightning or Channeled Dragonfire costs another {R}, for three mana total. Mage followed by Slick Sequence costs four mana and satisfies Sequence's draw condition because Mage was another spell cast that turn. Flashback permission expires that turn. Burst Lightning can also be kicked through flashback: Mage plus kicked Burst Lightning costs seven mana. Scalding Viper // Steam Clean is a creature card in the graveyard, so Mage cannot target it.
- **Spell order:** Burst Lightning followed by Slick Sequence costs {R} + {U}{R} = three mana and draws a card. Reversing that order does not draw unless another spell preceded Sequence. Vibrant Outburst can damage the opponent while tapping a blocker, converting two mana into both reach and an attack lane.
- **Channeled Dragonfire:** Harmonize starts at {5}{R}{R}; tapping a power-3 creature reduces it to {2}{R}{R}. Red requirements remain. That four-mana cast pumps Stallion but does not create a copy: actual mana spent must reach five. Tapping a power-2 creature leaves {3}{R}{R}, which does qualify. Weigh the saved mana against losing that creature's attack.
- **Hired Claw and Soulstone Sanctuary:** An animated Sanctuary is a Lizard, so its attack can trigger every Hired Claw even when the Claws stay home. Each Claw triggers once per attack, not once per attacking Lizard. After its trigger deals damage, {1}{R} can grow that Claw before combat damage.

## 3. The curve and sequencing by turn

There are 24 lands and only four true turn-one creatures, all Hired Claw. Spirebluff Canal is an excellent early land: it enters untapped as your first, second, or third land, but not your fourth. Riverpyre Verge always supplies red; its blue ability needs a Mountain or Island, including Steam Vents. Stormcarved Coast wants to be the third land or later. Soulstone Sanctuary supplies neither color.

**On the play:** Turn one, lead an untapped red source and Hired Claw. Turn two, attack, then add Scalding Viper or Stingcaster Mage; alternatively, grow Claw after its trigger when that improves combat. Turn three, deploy Colorstorm Stallion or warp Nova Hellkite for {2}{R}. Turn four, combine a creature with cheap interaction, or cast Smaug the Magnificent or Chandra, Torch of Defiance. Turn five, choose between a permanent Nova Hellkite for {3}{R}{R}, kicked Burst Lightning with Stallion, or several cheaper spells.

**On the draw:** Turn one may be Burst Lightning or Channeled Dragonfire instead of Claw. Turn two, remove or bounce a key blocker rather than force a weak attack. Turn three, haste can recover initiative, but retain interaction if racing is unfavorable. Turn four, Mage plus a cheap flashback spell can rebuild while answering something. Turn five, choose the stabilizing Hellkite body over warp when defense matters. These are branches, not a requirement to curve out blindly.

## 4. Mulligans

Keep most sevens with two or three lands, usable red early, a cheap threat, and interaction or a meaningful follow-up. Blue access by turn three matters when Colorstorm Stallion and the two-color spells dominate the hand. Four lands can be acceptable with strong early action and a durable payoff.

Ship hands whose spells begin at three mana without early interaction, hands overloaded with five-mana plans, and apparent two-land keeps that cannot cast their colored spells. Two Riverpyre Verge provide red but no blue without an Island or Mountain. A one-land hand needs exceptional cheap action and is particularly risky on the play. When reducing hand size, retain functional colors and early plays before redundant expensive threats.

## 5. Combat and defense

Attack aggressively when haste and removal produce favorable exchanges, but count crackback damage before committing. Stallion is not automatically large: spells must be cast before the relevant damage. Its ward discourages targeted interaction, not blocking or sweepers. Vibrant Outburst tapping a blocker before attackers is often worth more than its damage alone.

Hired Claw can attack into a trade when its attack trigger and burn establish lethal, but preserving it usually compounds damage over several turns. Scalding Viper's passive damage rewards survival; trading it for an insignificant attacker can surrender substantial reach. Stingcaster Mage is easier to trade after its entry value is used.

Warped Nova Hellkite disappears at the next end step, so do not count it as a blocker for the opponent's following turn. Hard-cast Hellkite can defend effectively. Sanctuary has vigilance, but activating it with mana from itself leaves it tapped and unable to attack that combat.

## 6. Resources and timing

Reserve {R} for Burst Lightning or {U} for Bounce Off; {U}{R} represents Slick Sequence or Vibrant Outburst. Steam Clean and Channeled Dragonfire require your main phase. Sequence only checks spells cast during the current turn, so last turn's activity is irrelevant.

Steam Clean costs {1}{U}, then Scalding Viper costs {1}{R} from exile later: four mana across two casts. Warp similarly provides temporary Hellkite pressure for three mana, followed by a five-mana cast from exile on a later turn. These are deferred threats, not free recursion.

Chandra, Torch of Defiance can supply {R}{R}, but cannot solve blue requirements. Her exile ability offers casting, not playing lands; an uncast land yields the damage instead. Smaug the Magnificent normally attacks for zero trigger damage on its first turn: its Treasure arrives at your upkeep. Spending that Treasure reduces later attack-trigger damage.

## 7. What beats it and how to adapt

Lifegain and large blockers punish racing; prioritize removing lifegain creatures and use Bounce Off or Steam Clean to create one decisive attack. Bounce is temporary and may repeat an opposing entry ability, so avoid using it merely to delay an unimportant threat.

Sweepers punish creature accumulation, especially Stallion tokens. Stagger threats, retain haste creatures, and use exile follow-ups after the battlefield clears. Soulstone Sanctuary remains a creature after activation, exposing a land to creature removal and sweepers. Graveyard hate weakens Mage and harmonize; spend available recursion before that window closes rather than stockpiling every burn spell.

Against faster aggro, protect life, minimize unnecessary Steam Vents payments, and trade small creatures when necessary. Against control, lean on haste, Scalding Viper, Chandra, Torch of Defiance, and damage from hand. No sideboard was supplied, so these adaptations concern sequencing and resource allocation.

## 8. Common mistakes

Avoid spending five mana across several spells and expecting a Stallion copy: one instant or sorcery must itself receive five mana. Do not give a new copy the original's temporary bonuses. Mockingbird checks mana spent against mana value: copying Stallion normally costs {2}{U}; copying Hellkite costs {4}{U}, even if Hellkite was warped for three.

Do not forget Claw's life-loss requirement or once-per-turn activation limit. Do not cast Mage without budgeting its same-turn flashback. Avoid late Spirebluff Canal land drops when an untapped alternative enables lethal, and never assume Riverpyre Verge supplies blue unaided.
