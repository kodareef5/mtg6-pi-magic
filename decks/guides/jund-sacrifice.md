# Jund Sacrifice

Written 2026-10-11 by gpt-6.1-sol:high from the list at https://www.mtggoldfish.com/deck/7974951, cards generated 2026-10-10. Sideboardless alpha: main deck only. Advice for the pregame; the board and card text win any disagreement.

## 1. Archetype and game plan

Jund Sacrifice is an engine-driven midrange deck: develop cheap creatures, turn expendable permanents into cards and mana, then win through sacrifice damage, enlarged attackers, or Rottenmouth Viper. It usually develops on turns one through three and threatens decisive swings from turn four onward rather than racing with ordinary creature damage.

The 60 cards contain 22 lands: 4 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1. Tokens bridge that relatively light mana base. Biotech Specialist and The Sackville-Bagginses make resource conversion hurt the opponent; Obsessive Pursuit makes the same sacrifices strengthen combat. Against removal, preserve recurring value rather than one oversized attacker.

This produces a card and Treasure, deals 2 damage, and causes 1 life loss. Sacrificing that Treasure later produces another 2 damage and 1 life loss if both payoffs remain. Greedy Freebooter sacrificed to The Sackville-Bagginses instead supplies a scry, a card, and two Treasures: one from each creature's trigger.
- **Free sacrifice outlet:** Umbral Collar Zealot costs {1}{B}; its ability costs no mana. Sacrifice spare artifacts to surveil and trigger payoffs without paying their activation costs. You forgo the Clue's draw, Lander's search, or Treasure's mana. Sacrificing Greedy Freebooter replaces it with a Treasure; sacrificing Callous Inspector creates a Clue but costs you 1 life.
- **Combat conversion:** Obsessive Pursuit costs {1}{B}. With it and Umbral Collar Zealot established, sacrifice Greedy Freebooter, its resulting Treasure, and a Clue: three permanents sacrificed gives the targeted attacker three counters and lifelink. Let the death trigger resolve before sacrificing its Treasure. X is determined when the attack trigger resolves, so sacrifices while that trigger is pending also count; sacrifices afterward do not enlarge that trigger's result.
- **Recursion:** Lively Dirge costs {2}{B} for searching only, {3}{B} for returning only, or {4}{B} for both. Both modes search first, then return. Two mana-value-two creatures fit because 2 + 2 = 4; Azog, Moria's Ruin plus a one-drop fits because 3 + 1 = 4. Returning Biotech Specialist with The Sackville-Bagginses lets you order their entry triggers so the Lander arrives before the sacrifice choice. Rottenmouth Viper cannot be returned. Mutagen Man, Living Ooze returns without Mutagen tokens.
- **Finishers:** Rottenmouth Viper costs {5}{B} minus one generic mana per nonland permanent sacrificed: three sacrifices leave {2}{B}, five leave {B}. Preserve your payoff creatures where possible. Sacrifices are paid before opponents can respond to the spell; a counterspell does not refund them. One Treasure cannot both produce mana and be sacrificed as Viper's additional cost. Its first trigger presents one payment decision; its next attack normally presents two, not automatic eight-life loss.

## 3. The curve and sequencing by turn

Lead Blooming Marsh for a black or green one-drop when possible. Starting Town is untapped only on your first three turns; Blooming Marsh depends on your number of other lands. Preserve untapped early windows. Overgrown Tomb and Blood Crypt enable Blazemire Verge's red ability through Swamp; Stomping Ground enables it through Mountain.

**On the play, a representative development:**
- **Turn one:** Gene Pollinator from Blooming Marsh.
- **Turn two:** Stomping Ground, paying life if necessary, then Biotech Specialist using both lands.
- **Turn three:** Add an untapped black source. Tap Gene Pollinator and the Lander for mana, cast The Sackville-Bagginses, and sacrifice that tapped Lander. Three lands plus Pollinator provide four mana, enough for Obsessive Pursuit too.
- **Turn four:** Deploy Umbral Collar Zealot, convert tokens before combat, and reserve {B} when interaction matters.
- **Turn five:** Choose a discounted Rottenmouth Viper, Lively Dirge recovery, or token activations rather than automatically casting everything.

**On the draw:** Turn one often favors Greedy Freebooter or Callous Inspector as a blocker. Turn two, use Requiting Hex if necessary instead of an engine. Turn three, establish Biotech Specialist or The Sackville-Bagginses behind that defense. Turn four, stabilize with removal or Wolverine, Fierce Fighter. Turn five, rebuild with Lively Dirge or turn accumulated fodder into Rottenmouth Viper. Delay Obsessive Pursuit when its life loss would undermine stabilization.

## 4. Mulligans

Keep two or three lands, an early creature, and a payoff or useful interaction. Check actual colors: black starts most hands, but Biotech Specialist needs both red and green, and Mutagen Man, Living Ooze needs double green.

Ship one-land hands that merely hope Gene Pollinator survives, hands without meaningful early plays, and colorless-heavy hands unable to cast their spells. A two-land hand with Pollinator, castable fodder, and an engine is stronger than a four-land hand of expensive finishers. Against aggression, prioritize a blocker and removal; against slower decks, prioritize sustainable value.

## 5. Combat and defense

Attack expendable creatures when trades feed death triggers or Lively Dirge. Callous Inspector's menace makes it a useful Obsessive Pursuit target; Mutagen Man, Living Ooze adds trample to a counter-heavy attack. Keep Biotech Specialist and The Sackville-Bagginses back when their continued triggers exceed likely combat damage.

Block with fodder, then sacrifice it to Umbral Collar Zealot before damage when preserving its death value matters. A blocked attacker without trample remains blocked even after its blocker disappears; trample can still hit you. Sacrificing your blocker also forfeits its combat damage.

Wolverine, Fierce Fighter can fight and then attack with haste. A later damage event heals previously marked damage, but one lethal event still kills him; deathtouch remains dangerous.

## 6. Resources and timing

Gene Pollinator must overcome summoning sickness before using its tap ability. Its second tapped permanent can be a newly entered creature, token, or enchantment. Tap a noncreature resource when possible to preserve attackers and blockers.

Keep {B} for Requiting Hex against creatures with mana value two or less. Blighting a 1/1 can trigger its death ability, but that death is not a sacrifice. Deadly Precision is sorcery-speed: budget {B} plus fodder, or {4}{B} without fodder.

Clue and Lander activations normally cost {2}; activate before combat when pursuing counters, or later when retaining flexibility matters. Only Forest and Swamp are basic search targets, one each. Fountainport's draw requires {2}, tapping the land, and a token, effectively committing three mana-producing lands if lands fund it. Track shock-land payments, Starting Town, Callous Inspector, and Obsessive Pursuit against your lifelink opportunities.

## 7. What beats it and how to adapt

Fast evasive pressure punishes setup turns and self-inflicted life loss. Cut optional value spending, trade early, and use removal before investing in counters. Wide boards absorb Rottenmouth Viper triggers cheaply; shrink their resources first when possible.

Exile removal denies death triggers and recursion. Graveyard hate weakens Lively Dirge, so shift toward live token engines and hard-cast threats. Sweepers punish overextension: retain a rebuilding creature or Dirge rather than deploying every payoff. Countermagic particularly punishes sacrifice-heavy Viper casts; lead with smaller value plays and avoid sacrificing your entire position.

An opponent should prioritize the active outlet or payoff that makes available fodder dangerous, and threaten Obsessive Pursuit's chosen attacker before its trigger resolves. No sideboard was supplied; these are sequencing adjustments, not prescribed swaps.

## 8. Common mistakes

- Counting a creature dying, blighting, or leaving through the legend rule as a sacrifice.
- Sacrificing payoffs unnecessarily before cashing the artifacts they reward.
- Expecting sacrifice costs back after a counterspell.
- Treating Rottenmouth Viper's blight counters as -1/-1 counters.
- Forgetting late tapped lands, Blazemire Verge's red restriction, or exhausted basic targets.
- Spending the only attacker on Gene Pollinator, then expecting Obsessive Pursuit to provide lifelink that turn.
