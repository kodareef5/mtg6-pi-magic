# Comparing ordinary pregame content

The tuning rounds held one carried brief nearly constant. Its policies, phase
defaults and card notes reach both planning and execution. Moving its worked
examples behind lookup did not isolate their absence: blocked-position sessions
retrieved them. The failed recognition gate also did not isolate a cause.
An ordinary new pregame is a smaller next test than a template language or
another staged writer.

This comparison changes content, not the playbook contract. It does not supply
winning lines, edit policy priorities by hand, change models or reinterpret cards.
A result on two new briefs cannot establish a general improvement in pregame.

Both seats get the existing four analysts and synthesis. Inputs contain no

## Construction comparison

The shared runner's `brief-content` arm accepts exactly two ordinary pregame
output files through repeated `--brief-source` arguments. It compares the
carried brief, A and B with three production `planWork` sessions each. Rotate
order as carried/A/B, A/B/carried, B/carried/A. No planner prompt, schema, lookup,
ceiling, reply budget or validation changes. Luna low remains the strategist.

Use only naturally fresh planning bases. The runner refuses an inherited
finite plan instead of clearing it. Verify that differences in exact first
requests are limited to the brief and its normally derived initial objective,
guidance and phase defaults. Card notes follow the ordinary brief reader.
Physical facts, accepted equipment, action keys, history and settings must match.
The default benchmark path remains unchanged.

First run `red-blocked-lethal-fresh`, completing all nine sessions before deciding.
Each fresh brief must produce three accepted plans with feasible winning
commitments against the visible blocking responses, assuming no intervening
hidden response. Accept any winning line. Require consistent delivered payment,
attack, waiting, target, trigger and completion policies. False audit rationale
is a separate quality defect, not silently a delivered instruction.

Both fresh briefs must pass all three and improve over the matched carried
control to advance. If all arms pass, this comparison establishes no improvement.
Any failed or unresolved fresh slot stops expansion after this complete batch.
A difference between A and B is an observation of brief variability, not a reason
to select the lucky replacement. Runner PASS means an accepted plan; historical
case checks remain diagnostic and do not require a particular winning attack set.

Only after a positive blocked batch, repeat the same nine-session comparison at
`red-upkeep-lethal`. Its winning commitments must exist at the tested acceptance;
a later draw writer cannot supply the missing finish. Again, any coherent winning
line qualifies, not only the known two-Strike line.

No physical continuation is supported by this arm yet. Positive construction at
both stages is required before implementing that next gate. Planner and pilot
must then receive the same documented experimental brief. An old pilot brief
would confound execution. Physical outcomes, legality, obedience, repair dependence,
gaps, fallback and replay/clone remain separate grades.

Ready-lethal, feasible Green development/defense, and held-out positions remain
required before a full game. This early stop does not waive them. No default
adoption or full-game claim follows from construction alone.

## Interpretation and accounting

A positive result says these frozen new briefs performed better on these cases.
It does not certify the current contract or establish that refreshing briefs
usually helps. If both fail, these two replacements did not repair construction;
that does not rule out other content or prove a staging mechanism.

Record preparation and planning separately: exact requests, accepted and refused
submissions, lookups, calls, token usage, cost, latency, truncation and failures.
Do not turn a failed or unmetered request into free successful work. Evidence lives
under `.pi/resume-20261007/brief-content/`.
