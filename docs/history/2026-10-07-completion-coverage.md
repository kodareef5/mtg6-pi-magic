# Writer completion coverage

The three [post-entry repairs](2026-10-07-post-entry.md) left their current main
window without an authored completion policy. Two selected winning attacks,
but neither established complete execution coverage. The writer's syntax check
accepts that omission, while Jev's contract says missing policy grants no pass.

The opt-in `completion` benchmark arm checks the fully expanded plan. It uses
existing `phases[].when` and `complete: pass | ask`; it adds no core syntax,
stored progress, generated default, physical action or planner session.
The ordinary writer, pregame defaults and historical journals remain unchanged.

## Required scope

- A turn plan covers the current and remaining normal priority windows through
  its context-stamped `throughTurn`. Current-turn steps come from the projected
  remaining schedule, including inserted or repeated steps. Future turns use
  the ordinary schedule, not a prediction of effects or surviving objects.
- Preparation starts at its labelled next upkeep and covers the following turn.
- A narrow opponent-turn response repair checks only its current window. It
  retains unaffected policies without demanding a replacement next-turn plan.
- Untap and ordinary cleanup have no priority requirement. An actual current
  cleanup priority decision does need coverage.

An authored broad `when` can cover several windows. Matching `pass` and `ask`
policies conflict and are reported together; no precedence is inferred. The
validator reports every uncovered window in one refusal and never chooses
either policy. `ask` is valid syntax, including a broad request for help. Its
usefulness is graded separately. Coverage certifies neither response decisions
nor strategic quality.

False conditions remain false only at the current position, with no execution
credit. Available branches, unavailable required actions, stack prerequisites,
matching marks, physical options and ordering remain unchanged. This arm does
not change the pilot renderer or teach it a new completion rule. A pass while
the stack is nonempty lets resolution continue; it does not prove a step or
window finished. The seat still applies the authored response policy.

## Preferred execution

For Mountain, Zhao and an established Kellan, the writer names the land, cast,
attack and declaration finish, plus its chosen completion in the surrounding
windows. Jev pays, waits for resolution and chooses the passes and declaration.
An empty postcombat action list still needs the writer's completion choice.

For Hydra, a land and a fetch, the writer retains structural stack waits, trigger
targets, search instructions and response exceptions. Authored completion does
not authorize skipping a live response branch or an unavailable required step.
If a condition becomes true after resolution, the next question reads it again.
The code neither ends the phase nor asks a planner automatically.

### Obedience, outcomes and limits

All four raw wins preserved the initial winning attack set after a Red repair.
The recorded Explorer block on Kellan was legal; Claw dealt one to Green and
Sanctuary plus Challenger dealt five. No win was free of Red repair, and neither
arm earned an all-seat clean win. The two stopped runs have no outcome; they
are neither losses nor unresolved writer replies.

There were no Red available-branch pass-over exposures. The initial Red plans
have no branches, so that guard cannot establish response obedience. Green in
treatment 1 passed one available optional creature branch. The separate ordered
development step postponed its action until after combat; the branch remained
discretionary. Its pass is an observation, not an automatic violation. Green's
later persistent Harmonizer cast followed combat. The delivered main policy

The one exposed Red defensive policy occurred in treatment 1 after its repair:
Red blocked Explorer with Challenger, then finished, following the phase prose
despite a marked finish step and explicit `pass` completion. No skipped required
block was observed there. The four wins ended before defensive play, and control
1 stopped before Green's combat. Those boundaries leave most defensive and
response coverage untested.

Both run-1 repairs added a futile Kellan Detective activation. Jev paid the two
Mountains as instructed and waited for resolution. Kellan was already a Rogue,
so the accepted instruction's Scout condition was false and resolution skipped

waiting failure. Control 1 also ordered a second upgrade for three mana after
spending both Mountains, leaving only Sanctuary. That step was unavailable;
Red passed after resolution and left the window without completing it or asking
for help. Record both the infeasible repaired commitment and that execution
omission. The accepted structured condition is available for a later context
audit; this experiment adds no effect forecast or recommendation.

Repairs also introduced delivered false claims. Control 0 promoted its false

resolution guidance. Sanctuary was a Lizard. The sole-target announcement was

All six had zero recorded gaps, fallback and judge attempts, matched replay and
matched their cloned source prefix. These checks establish run health, not
