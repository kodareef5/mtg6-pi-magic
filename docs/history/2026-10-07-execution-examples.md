# Execution questions to cover before changing the default

These are preferred uses for the next execution change, not a description of a
shipped schema. The candidate experiment only renders three own-turn windows.
Its physical wins do not establish delivery of every selected policy.

The planner owns choices: actions, targets, response conditions, holds and their
release, completion, and exceptions. Context derives order, waiting and recorded
progress. Jev chooses every voluntary action and pass. Rationale is an audit
record, not another source of instructions or facts for either Jev or a later
turn's planner.

## Land, cast and attack

Red reaches draw with one untapped Mountain, Mountain and Zhao in hand, an
established Kellan, and no opposing creature. The chosen line is Mountain,
Zhao, then attack with Kellan. The response policy supplies no current response;
the planner explicitly permits ending each window after its commitments finish.

1. At draw priority, Jev sees that the first action requires main phase and the
   applicable response policy permits passing to it. An unrelated instant is
   still offered. No code chooses the pass.
2. In main, the first question names the land play. After its ledger row, the
   question names Zhao. Payment facts show two Mountains spent and none left.
   A forecast of this payment is not a fixed choice unless the planner explicitly
   chose one. No prose claims that a spent Mountain remains available.
3. With Zhao on the stack, the question names the waiting cast and response
   policy. Passing permits resolution; it is not proof the cast resolved.
4. At attackers, Kellan is selected. Zhao is sick and not selected. If two
   attackers had been selected, their order would not rank them. Jev ends the
   declaration only under the explicit completion policy.
5. Postcombat reads its own commitments and completion policy. A missing or
   empty action list creates no permission to pass. An uncovered card revealed
   by a combat-damage trigger gets its own narrow question.

A newly tapped attacker or new blocker invalidates the relevant commitment;
the old instruction does not override current characteristics or legal options.

Before choosing a line, strategy receives the existing damage arithmetic for
each current creature, unblocked and against each single opposing blocker.
A double striker blocked by a creature deals no player damage without trample,
even if first strike kills the blocker. Keyword conflicts and tapped blockers
remain explicit. These are separate hypothetical pairs, not a whole combat or
a prediction of triggers, responses, future entries or chosen blocks. The
planner must still choose its attackers and account for creatures left idle.

## Beneficiary, fetch, targets and search

Green controls a sick Explorer, two Ascensions and three untapped Forests, with
Hydra in hand and one permitted graveyard land play left. The chosen line casts
Hydra, plays Passage from the graveyard and activates Passage for a basic Forest.
The planner explicitly selects Hydra for the Ascension rewards and names the
trigger order it wants. It explicitly chooses no attackers and permits finishing
that declaration.

1. Jev casts Hydra using current sources. After resolution, Passage becomes the
   next action. Neither action is marked complete merely because it was offered.
2. Landfall presents ordering and target questions. Their instructions must be
   visible at announcement, before targets are locked. A later resolution
   purpose cannot repair a target already selected incorrectly.
3. The land play and Passage activation explicitly use `waitFor: "empty-stack"`.
   While landfall remains on the stack, the activation is physically available
   but its commitment reads waiting. Once the stack empties, Jev activates the
   entered Passage. The source is the same physical card with
   a new incarnation. The instruction for finding Forest belongs to that use.
4. During the paid search, Jev reads that original instruction even if another
   plan has since been accepted. Choosing none means finding nothing; it cannot
   undo the sacrifice or postpone paying. A deliberate empty search needs the
   seat's explicit policy.
5. After search and triggers resolve, current facts supply actual untapped
   sources, counters and creatures. The plan never presents its intended entries
   as though their effects already happened. Explorer and Hydra remain unable to
   attack unless current facts establish otherwise; sickness does not stop
   blocking on Red's turn.

If Explorer leaves before the graveyard play, its permission is gone. If Hydra
leaves, its target policy needs the stated alternate or a request for help.
Neither exception grants an automatic pass or a replacement line.

An amendment retains the prerequisite on an unfinished step, including when it
becomes the first step of the amended plan. A new response that must go above
an existing spell omits it. Two ordered responses can deliberately stack;
announcement order alone never implies waiting for resolution. Taking a waiting
action still records what the seat did, and fails the obedience check.

## Upkeep, responses and the opponent's turn

A prepared turn starts at upkeep after normal untap. Before any upkeep choice
or compulsory trigger announcement, a seat with prior scoped tactical work
accepts or amends that preparation against current facts. Opening and unscoped
legacy work still use pregame upkeep guidance before their post-draw deadline.
The background job is awaited, never replaced by a competing writer. A new
blocker prevents accepting its old attack policy unchanged.

In the frozen Red upkeep, Green has 6 life. Red has two Lightning Strikes and
four untapped lands, three Mountains and Sanctuary. The supplied line casts
both Strikes at Green, waiting for the first to resolve before casting the
second. Each costs two mana; together they deal six. Jev follows that payment
and target policy before any Sanctuary animation or draw. Winning here ends
the game before a draw amendment is needed.

An upkeep fetch instead continues through payment, search, landfall targets and
resolution under the accepted policy. The draw amendment reads the sacrificed
source, fetched land, counters and remaining mana. It removes completed upkeep
steps, retains unfinished stack prerequisites and the same expiry, and preserves
the opponent-turn policy. It never reinstalls the original preparation. Accepting
a plan at upkeep alone does not establish coverage for the later draw.

A response reserve names the response, source, useful window and release.
For example, a Veil branch depends on Veil being in hand and meaningful targeted
interaction. An absent Veil releases that reserve. An unfunded optional branch
is visible as unfunded; it neither refuses a useful ordered line nor invents
mana. Casting a response still uses a listed action with actual targets/payment.

Blocking uses current attackers, eligible blockers and damage facts under the
prepared defense policy. A sick creature may block. No old attack forecast or
claim about a nonexistent animated land reaches this question as fact. If the
chosen policy does not cover a changed threat, Jev asks for that missing choice.

Cleanup remains a compulsory discard question with its retention policy. Empty
main-phase instructions do not answer it. Opening, trigger ordering, resolution,
priority and declarations must remain different questions even when they share
the same rules step.

## What can carry into the next plan

Fresh preparation carries reusable pregame policies, accepted card equipment
and current facts. Only amendments within the same valid scope carry branches,
holds and unfinished commitments with their actual status.
An old statement that Kellan already attacked, Red has six life, or a land is
animated is a bound conclusion from an earlier position. It must not become the
next turn's authority simply because the writer omitted a prose edit. After
expiry, scheduled acceptance supplies new upkeep policy. Until acceptance, only
applicable standing policy remains; absent coverage grants no pass.

Any prepared-plan skip needs positive coverage for the observed change. The
first case remains an ordinary opposing land change plus a drawn land covered
by the prepared branch, with the response and combat conditions still valid.
A changed blocker is the counterexample. Syntactic validity, a payable line and
an empty stop list do not establish that coverage.

## Completing a partial attacker declaration

Kellan is already selected. The accepted remaining steps name Sanctuary, Zhao
and then finishing. Both attackers remain offered. Their order is immaterial.
The finish option should state which currently available plan steps finishing
would leave unfinished, naming their offered attack choices. It stays available
and retains its normal execution credit if selected. This is a fact about the
accepted plan, not a requirement imposed by the table.

After either attacker is selected, the mark names only the other unfinished
step. Once both are selected, it disappears. False conditions, completed steps,
unavailable attackers and optional branches do not create this mark. A broad
step that itself matches finishing cannot be labelled unfinished by that action.
Jev must choose both attacks before finishing; displaying the mark does not
prove obedience. Repeat the saved partial-declaration continuation unchanged
before and after adding the mark, with outcomes and help counted separately.
